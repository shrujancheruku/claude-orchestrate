# Model routing and cost: evidence for orchestrating AI coding agents

Research ticket #607, part of the evidence map #588. Area prefix `RT`. Sources checked 2026-11-10 against the primary paper, leaderboard or vendor page; anything only seen through a secondary report is marked "(secondary)".

**Summary.** The evidence is strong that cost is a first-class axis of agent quality: cost-controlled evaluation finds the most expensive model on the accuracy-cost frontier in only 1 of 9 agent benchmarks (HAL, 21,730 rollouts), and simple cheap-first escalation beats elaborate agent architectures on HumanEval at a fraction of the cost. For coding agents specifically, three 2026 studies agree that more reasoning effort is not monotonically better: on a hard SWE-bench Verified slice, Opus 5 at low effort was on the cost-success frontier and xhigh cost 2.4 to 4.5 times more for no significant gain, while every timeout and empty patch came at high or xhigh. Per-role heterogeneity is supported but not simple: a strong planner with a cheap executor beat strong-model self-play on SWE-bench by 8 points at $0.021 per task, yet two equal-cost studies found planner and critic roles add nothing over a single agent, so the role split must be measured, not assumed. Routers that read only the task description barely beat chance on multi-turn SWE tasks (AUROC 0.53 to 0.57); routers that watch the first turns of a cheap worker, or a trained router on Opus-class pools, cut API cost 53% at an equal resolve rate. Prompt caching is the largest single lever that is not a model choice: cache reads are 0.05x to 0.1x the input price on current Claude models, caching cut a coding agent's bill by about 61% with behaviour unchanged, and a mid-conversation model switch forfeits the cache entirely, which argues for hand-offs to fresh subagents rather than in-place downgrades. Thin or contradictory: no study measures per-role model assignment for a reviewer role; the cost-per-resolved-issue leaderboards disagree by scaffold and do not count caching; and the 2026 routing papers are preprints with small held-out sets (25 to 100 instances).

## Sources

### 1. Weinberger & Hozez (2026), Prompt-Induced Waste in Coding Agents

- **Citation:** Weinberger, S., & Hozez, A. (2026). Prompt-Induced Waste in Coding Agents: Reasoning, Effort, Harness Design, and End-to-End Cost. Preprint, arXiv:2608.01347 (v6, 10 Sep 2026), CC BY 4.0. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.01347
- **Design:** preregistered paired comparisons (McNemar, Holm correction) across prompt wording, effort level and harness; the SWE-bench campaign is 150 model-task-effort cells. Defines efficiency as cost per successful task.
- **Population:** Claude Sonnet 5 and Claude Opus 5 through Claude Code 2.1.220 on a frozen hard slice of SWE-bench Verified: 25 instances whose gold patch touches at least two files and 40 lines, at most four per repository, chosen from structural metadata only. Harness comparison: Claude Code vs PI.DEV vs a DeepSeek harness, with six open-weight reasoning models plus Sonnet 5.
- **Headline results (Appendix N):** Opus 5 resolved 17/25 at low, 18/25 at high, 16/25 at xhigh (xhigh vs low McNemar p = 1.0); cost per resolved task $0.75 (low), $1.89 (high), $3.62 (xhigh). Sonnet 5 resolved 12/25 at low, 19/25 at high, 17/25 at xhigh; high vs low had seven rescues and no regressions (p = 0.016), xhigh vs low was not significant after Holm correction; cost per resolved task $1.76, $2.32, $3.00. Across the campaign xhigh vs low added 12 to 16k thinking tokens and 23 to 30 turns and raised billed cost 2.4 to 4.5x. All six timeouts and all four empty patches occurred at high or xhigh. Harness (Section 6): Claude Code vs PI.DEV on matched triples had a 12 to 15x larger fixed prefix and 2 to 7x more turns, giving 5 to 30x higher cost per success at comparable success rates; about half of Claude Code's calls were test executions vs 22% under PI.DEV. Cache (Appendix E.4): provider-side prefix caching cut the bill by about 61% (billed cost about 39% of the no-cache figure) with behavioural metrics unchanged; fresh Claude Code sessions often got first-turn hits of about 90% of a 16 to 20k prefix; changing the working directory broke reuse because the harness embeds it in the system prompt.
- **Limitations:** N = 25 per cell, so a two-task swing moves a rate by 8 points; one harness version; Claude-only for the effort campaign; preprint.
- **For orchestrate:** the direct evidence for the map #513 question "which effort per role": for an Opus worker, low effort sat on the frontier; for Sonnet, high beat low significantly but xhigh did not. Reviewer L at `effort: high` is defensible; anything at xhigh is not. The 61% caching figure and the working-directory cache break both bear on how workers are dispatched into worktrees.

### 2. Kapoor et al. (2025), Holistic Agent Leaderboard (HAL)

- **Citation:** Kapoor, S., Stroebl, B., Kirgis, P., Nadgir, N., Siegel, Z. S., Wei, B., Xue, T., Chen, Z., Chen, F., Utpala, S., Ndzomga, F., Oruganty, D., Luskin, S., Liu, K., Yu, B., Arora, A., Hahm, D., Trivedi, H., Sun, H., Lee, J., Jin, T., Mai, Y., Zhou, Y., Zhu, Y., Bommasani, R., Kang, D., Song, D., Henderson, P., Su, Y., Liang, P., & Narayanan, A. (2025). Holistic Agent Leaderboard: The Missing Infrastructure for AI Agent Evaluation. Preprint, arXiv:2510.11977. https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.11977 ; leaderboard https://hal.cs.princeton.edu/
- **Design:** standardised harness, 21,730 agent rollouts across 9 models and 9 benchmarks (coding, web, science, customer service), about $40,000 of API spend, all logs released (2.5B tokens).
- **Population:** Claude Opus 4.1, Sonnet 4, 3.7 Sonnet, o4-mini, GPT-5, Gemini 2.0 Flash, DeepSeek R1 and others; SWE-bench Verified Mini, USACO, CORE-Bench, Online Mind2Web, ScienceAgentBench, tau-bench and more.
- **Headline results (Section 4.1):** "in 21 of 36 model-agent-benchmark combinations, increased reasoning effort produces equal or lower accuracy." The most expensive model (Opus 4.1 at $15/$75 per MTok) was on the accuracy-cost frontier in only 1 of 9 benchmarks; on average fewer than one third of models sit on the frontier for a benchmark; Gemini 2.0 Flash was on it for 7 of 9, GPT-5 for 4 of 9, o4-mini Low for 4 of 9, DeepSeek R1 for none. Scaffold choice moved cost 9x for about two accuracy points on Online Mind2Web ($171 SeeAct + GPT-5 Medium vs $1,577 Browser-Use + Sonnet 4). The task-specific SWE-Agent scaffold beat the generalist scaffold on SWE-bench Verified Mini in 11 of 12 runs. On the SWE-bench Verified Mini leaderboard (read 2026-11-10), SWE-Agent + Claude Sonnet 4.5 High reached 72.00% at $463.90 total, HAL Generalist + Claude Haiku 4.5 High 44.00% at $65.31, SWE-Agent + Gemini 2.0 Flash 24.00% at $4.72; the page notes costs are "calculated without accounting for caching benefits" and a 2026-10-05 notice that some rows are overstated 2x by double-counted spans.
- **Limitations:** 2025 model generation; the reasoning-effort finding gives no cost deltas; leaderboard costs exclude caching and carry a known overstatement.
- **For orchestrate:** the strongest multi-benchmark statement that higher effort is not a safe default and that the priciest model is rarely cost-optimal. It supports the map's instinct to trial a Sonnet orchestrator and medium (not high) effort as defaults, with measurement rather than assumption.

### 3. Jiang et al. (2026), Specialize Roles, Mix Deployments

- **Citation:** Jiang, Y., Cheng, L., Huang, Y., Zhao, Y., Lu, Z., Li, W., Ponti, E., Dong, L., & Mai, L. (2026). Specialize Roles, Mix Deployments: Pushing the Cost-Accuracy Frontier of LLM Agent Teams. Preprint, arXiv:2606.20629 (28 May 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.20629
- **Design:** benchmark ablation of all 16 planner-executor pairings of four API models, plus self-hosted open-weight models, across five domains; a cost-matched "synergy" measure against budget-mixed homogeneous teams; a three-role (planner, verifier, executor) extension.
- **Population:** GPT-5.4, Claude Opus 4.6, MiniMax-M2.7, GLM-5.1; Qwen3.5-4B/27B, GPT-OSS-20B/120B; MCP-Atlas, MedAgentBench, FinanceBench, IMO-AnswerBench, SWE-bench.
- **Headline results:** heterogeneous teams "improve accuracy by up to 44% when models are assigned to their stronger roles" and reversing the pair "can reduce accuracy by 36%" (both extremes on SWE-bench; cost-matched synergy, not raw accuracy). SWE-bench (Table 4): GPT-5.4 planning + MiniMax-M2.7 executing scored 76.2% vs 68.2% for GPT-5.4 self-play, at $0.021 per task (Appendix B.5). The executor's median per-task dollar cost is about 6.3x the planner's overall (2.1x to 13.8x by benchmark) because it reads far more input tokens. Three roles on MCP-Atlas: GLM-5.1 planner, GPT-5.4 verifier, MiniMax-M2.7 executor reached 83.8% vs 80.4% for the best two-role team.
- **Limitations:** preprint; the 44%/36% figures are a derived synergy metric; the SWE-bench $0.021 per task is far below any leaderboard figure and the subset and scaffold need checking in the full text; no reviewer role is modelled.
- **For orchestrate:** the best direct evidence that a strong planner over a cheap executor is a real paradigm for coding, and that the direction matters (a weak planner over a strong executor can lose). It also says the executor should dominate cost; in orchestrate's 2026-11-07 baseline the orchestrator (planner) was $0.70 of $1.36 per merged ticket, the inverse, which argues the orchestrator's token load, not the worker model, is the first thing to fix.

### 4. Yang et al. (2026), TwinRouterBench

- **Citation:** Yang, P., Chen, W., Yang, T., Feng, P., Xing, J., Guo, W., Yao, Y., Han, Y., Li, H., Wang, X., Gao, Y., Wang, Z., Xiao, J., Yang, A., Tian, L., Ai, L., Yang, E., & Shi, T. (2026). TwinRouterBench: Fast Static and Live Dynamic Evaluation for Realistic Agentic LLM Routing. Preprint, arXiv:2605.18859 (v5, 24 Oct 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18859
- **Design:** a static track of 970 router-visible prefixes from 520 instances (SWE-bench, BFCL, mtRAG, QMSum, PinchBench) with execution-verified target tiers, and a dynamic track that runs routers live on SWE-bench Verified with a 100-instance held-out evaluation.
- **Population:** Claude Opus 4.6 as the unrouted baseline; a locked tiered pool including Haiku 4.5 in the mid_high tier; held-out set 18 Astropy + 82 Django instances, "not a random sample".
- **Headline results (Table 3):** Opus 4.6 unrouted 74/100 at $0.55 average API cost ($54.73 total); trained UncommonRoute 75/100 at $0.26 ($25.66), "1 - 25.66/54.73 = 53.1%" cheaper "at comparable resolve rate"; SR-KNN router 75/100 at $0.56 (no saving); a rule-based router 73/100 at $1.73 (3x worse than no routing).
- **Limitations:** 100 held-out instances from two repositories; preprint; the pool's members in the dynamic track are not listed in the sections read.
- **For orchestrate:** routing can halve cost at equal resolve rate, but a naive rule can triple it. Orchestrate's size/risk-path rule is the rule-based kind and should be checked against the baseline before it is widened (map #513's Haiku-for-every-non-risk-ticket decision).

### 5. Kapoor et al. (2024), AI Agents That Matter

- **Citation:** Kapoor, S., Stroebl, B., Siegel, Z. S., Nadgir, N., & Narayanan, A. (2024). AI Agents That Matter. Preprint, arXiv:2407.01502. https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502
- **Design:** cost-controlled re-evaluation of published agent architectures against simple baselines, means over five runs; survey of 17 agent benchmarks for holdout sets.
- **Population:** GPT-4 and GPT-3.5 (April 2024) on HumanEval (164 problems); LATS, LDB, Reflexion vs retry, warming and escalation baselines.
- **Headline results (Table A1):** Escalation, which "progressively escalated unsolved problems up a model chain of increasing cost" when a solution fails the example tests, reached 85.0% at $0.27 total, vs LDB (GPT-3.5) 80.2% at $0.63, GPT-4 zero-shot 89.6% at $1.93, Warming (GPT-4) 93.2% at $2.45, LATS (GPT-4) 88.0% at $134.50. "State-of-the-art" agent architectures did not outperform the simple baselines; LATS cost over 50 times more than warming. On NovelQA, RAG matched long-context accuracy at "more than 20 times less" cost. The majority of 17 surveyed benchmarks had no appropriate holdout.
- **Limitations:** HumanEval is saturated and contaminated for 2024 models; single-call tasks, not repository agents; costs are 2024 list prices.
- **For orchestrate:** the canonical argument for cheap-first escalation gated by a test, which is exactly orchestrate's Haiku worker with one Sonnet escalation after a failing round. It also sets the measurement standard: report accuracy and dollars together, on a held-out set.

### 6. Cuadron et al. (2025), The Danger of Overthinking

- **Citation:** Cuadron, A., Li, D., Ma, W., Wang, X., Wang, Y., Zhuang, S., Liu, S., Schroeder, L. G., Xia, T., Mao, H., Thumiger, N., Desai, A., Stoica, I., Klimovic, A., Neubig, G., & Gonzalez, J. E. (2025). The Danger of Overthinking: Examining the Reasoning-Action Dilemma in Agentic Tasks. Preprint, arXiv:2502.08235. https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08235
- **Design:** 4,018 SWE-bench Verified trajectories scored for "overthinking" (analysis paralysis, rogue actions, premature disengagement), regression of resolve rate on the score, and a selection intervention.
- **Population:** 19 models including o1, o1-mini, GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet, DeepSeek-R1, DeepSeek-V3, QwQ-32B, Qwen2.5 and R1-Distill families; OpenHands scaffold.
- **Headline results:** resolve rate falls with overthinking score, beta = -7.894 (R^2 = 0.892) for reasoning models and -15.938 (R^2 = 0.839, p = 0.010) for non-reasoning models. o1 low effort resolved 21.0% at $400; o1 high effort 29.1% at $1,400 (3.5x). Two low-effort samples, keeping the one with the lower overthinking score, reached 27.3% at $800 (43% below high); three samples reached 30.3%, above high effort, for $200 less. Overthinking rises as model size falls.
- **Limitations:** 2025 models; the overthinking score is LLM-judged; the intervention needs a scorer in the loop.
- **For orchestrate:** two cheap attempts plus a selector can match one expensive attempt; and smaller models overthink more, so a Haiku worker needs a tighter turn or time budget, not a looser one.

### 7. Bai et al. (2026), How Do AI Agents Spend Your Money?

- **Citation:** Bai, L., Huang, Z., Wang, X., Sun, J., Mihalcea, R., Brynjolfsson, E., Pentland, A., & Pei, J. (2026). How Do AI Agents Spend Your Money? Analyzing and Predicting Token Consumption in Agentic Coding Tasks. Preprint, arXiv:2604.22750 (v3, 2 Oct 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.22750
- **Design:** token-level analysis of 500 SWE-bench Verified instances, four independent runs each, eight models (OpenHands).
- **Population:** Claude Sonnet 3.7, Sonnet 4, Sonnet 4.5, GPT-5, GPT-5.2, Qwen3-Coder-480B-A35B, Kimi-K2, Gemini 3 Pro.
- **Headline results:** "input tokens, not output tokens, dominate the overall cost in agentic coding"; cache-read tokens are the largest cost contributor in every phase even though output is priced roughly 80x higher. Accuracy "peaks at the intermediate-cost run and then saturates with higher costs" (Figure 3). Runs on the same task differ by up to 30x in tokens (average most-to-least ratio about 2x). About 50% of file actions for Qwen3-Coder, Sonnet 4 and Kimi-K2 are repeat views or edits of the same file. Kimi-K2 and Sonnet 4.5 used on average over 1.5M more tokens than GPT-5 on the same tasks. Models predict their own token use poorly (best correlation 0.39) and "consistently underestimate".
- **Limitations:** one scaffold; abstract and body disagree on the exact multipliers (1000x vs 3500x) and on "saturates" vs "degrades"; preprint.
- **For orchestrate:** cost is a context-size problem before it is a model problem, so the orchestrator's and reviewers' prefix sizes and cache hit rates deserve instrumentation before the model table is tuned; and a worker's own cost estimate is not a routing signal.

### 8. Son et al. (2026), SWE-Router

- **Citation:** Son, S., Yoon, S., Tang, J., Wang, S., Wolf, L., & Bogunovic, I. (2026). SWE-Router: Routing in Multi-turn Agentic Software Engineering Tasks. 5th Deep Learning for Code Workshop, ICML 2026; arXiv:2607.00053. https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00053
- **Design:** a router that runs the weak model for K turns, scores the partial trajectory with a value head, and either lets the weak model continue or restarts the strong model from the task description; compared with prompt-only embedding routers on threshold-free AUROC and Route-AUC.
- **Population:** weak gpt-5-mini or deepseek-v3.2, strong gemini-3-pro-preview, mini-SWE-agent with a 75-step budget on SWE-bench Verified.
- **Headline results (Tables 1 and 2):** full-run baselines: gpt-5-mini 55.6% at $16.6 for 500 instances, deepseek-v3.2 62.4% at $22.6, gemini-3-pro-preview 70.4% at $391.3. Prompt-only routers (embedding + LR/kNN/XGB) reached AUROC 0.477 to 0.567, near chance; SWE-Router K=2 reached Route-AUC 0.780 for deepseek -> gemini, +15.3 pp over the non-temporal baseline.
- **Limitations:** no dollar cost or resolve rate reported for the routed system, only normalised curves; workshop paper.
- **For orchestrate:** the task text alone does not predict which ticket needs the strong model; the first few turns of a cheap worker do. This favours the existing escalate-after-failure design over any pre-dispatch classifier, and suggests the escalation trigger could fire earlier (after a stop or an idle nudge) rather than after a full failed round.

### 9. Bhola, Krishnan & NS (2026), Scrouting

- **Citation:** Bhola, I., Krishnan, A., & NS, M. (2026). Scrouting: Cost-Aware Routing of Coding Agents by Scouting the Repository First. Preprint, arXiv:2608.04804 (5 Aug 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.04804
- **Design:** a 7B searcher (SuperScout-7B) explores the repository and writes a handoff; a router then sends the task to one of four frontier fixers; ablations against each fixer alone and against always-cheapest-fixer-with-handoff.
- **Population:** the full Python slice of SWE-bench Pro, 266 tasks, official capped budget tier.
- **Headline results:** 159 of 266 solved vs 158 for the best single model, at "about a fifth of the total cost per solve"; the searcher adds under half a cent of GPU time per task. The ablation that always uses the cheapest fixer with the handoff ties the routed system, so the authors credit the handoff, not the routing decision.
- **Limitations:** preprint; fixers unnamed in the abstract; one benchmark slice.
- **For orchestrate:** a cheap scout's written handoff did the work the router was credited with. Orchestrate's touch map and brief are that handoff; making the scout's map richer is a cheaper lever than a smarter model choice.

### 11. Erol et al. (2025), Cost-of-Pass

- **Citation:** Erol, M. H., El, B., Suzgun, M., Yuksekgonul, M., & Zou, J. (2025). Cost-of-Pass: An Economic Framework for Evaluating Language Models. Preprint, arXiv:2504.13359 (v2, 26 Feb 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.13359
- **Design:** defines cost-of-pass v(m,p) = C_m(p) / R_m(p), the expected dollar cost of one correct solution, and the frontier cost-of-pass across models and a human expert; evaluates model classes and inference-time techniques.
- **Population:** lightweight (Llama-3.1-8B, GPT-4o mini, Llama-3.3-70B), large (Llama-3.1-405B, Claude Sonnet 3.5, GPT-4o), reasoning (o1-mini, o1, o3-mini, DeepSeek-R1) on Two-Digit Addition, GSM8K, BBQ, GPQA-Diamond, MATH-500, AIME-24.
- **Headline results:** lightweight models have the lowest cost-of-pass on basic quantitative tasks, large models on knowledge tasks (BBQ: GPT-4o 6.2e-3, Sonnet 3.5 6.4e-3), reasoning models on complex quantitative tasks (MATH-500: DeepSeek-R1 0.21, o1-mini 0.50). Frontier cost-of-pass for MATH-500 halved about every 2.6 months (May 2024 to Feb 2025), 7.1 months for AIME-24. Majority voting (k = 3, 4) gave zero relative economic gain on every dataset; self-refinement helped only on knowledge tasks (6.7% BBQ, 24.9% GPQA).
- **Limitations:** no coding or agentic tasks; single-turn.
- **For orchestrate:** cost per merged ticket is already orchestrate's metric and it is the right one; majority voting of whole workers is not worth its cost; and because the frontier moves monthly, any per-role model table should carry a review date.

### 12. Brown et al. (2024), Large Language Monkeys, and Snell et al. (2024), compute-optimal test-time scaling

- **Citations:** Brown, B., Juravsky, J., Ehrlich, R., Clark, R., Le, Q. V., Re, C., & Mirhoseini, A. (2024). Large Language Monkeys: Scaling Inference Compute with Repeated Sampling. Preprint, arXiv:2407.21787 (v3, Dec 2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.21787 ; Snell, C., Lee, J., Xu, K., & Kumar, A. (2024). Scaling LLM Test-Time Compute Optimally can be More Effective than Scaling Model Parameters. Preprint, arXiv:2408.03314. https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.03314
- **Design:** repeated-sampling coverage curves with cost accounting (Brown); FLOPs-matched comparison of test-time strategies (Snell).
- **Population:** DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct, GPT-4o, Claude 3.5 Sonnet in the Moatless Tools agent on SWE-bench Lite (Brown); PaLM 2-S* on MATH (Snell).
- **Headline results:** SWE-bench Lite coverage with DeepSeek-Coder-V2-Instruct rose from 15.9% at one sample to 56% at 250 samples. Table 1: five DeepSeek attempts at $0.0072 each solved 29.62% for $10.8 total, vs one GPT-4o attempt 24.00% at $0.13 each ($39, 3.6x) and one Claude 3.5 Sonnet attempt 26.70% at $0.17 each ($51, 4.7x). Selection is the bottleneck: on MATH coverage reached 98.44% at 10,000 samples while majority voting and reward-model picking rose only from 40.50% to 41.41%. Snell: a compute-optimal per-prompt allocation improves test-time scaling efficiency by more than 4x over best-of-N and, FLOPs-matched, "can outperform a 14x larger model" on problems where the small model has non-trivial success rates.
- **Limitations:** 2024 models; coverage counts any correct sample, which only pays where a verifier (tests, CI) exists; Snell is not a coding study.
- **For orchestrate:** repeated cheap attempts beat one strong attempt only where a trustworthy verifier picks the winner; orchestrate has one (CI plus the reviewer), so a "two Haiku attempts, CI picks" flag is evidence-backed, while a vote among attempts is not.

### 13. Dylan et al. (2026), equal-cost multi-agent structure, and Fu et al. (2026), Do More Agents Help?

- **Citations:** Dylan, D., Brennan, A., Murphy, C., O'Sullivan, N., Kelly, C., & Walsh, S. (2026). At Equal Inference Cost, Multi-Agent Structure Does Not Beat a Single Frozen Agent. Preprint, arXiv:2609.04217. https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.04217 ; Fu, Y., Fang, R., Shao, J., Zheng, H., Zhu, Z., Luo, B., Wen, Z., & Lin, T. (2026). Do More Agents Help? Evaluating Accuracy and Cost in LLM Multi-Agent Workflows. Preprint, arXiv:2606.05670 (v2, 9 Oct 2026). https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.05670
- **Design:** Dylan: planner-executor-critic prompts evolved by coordinate ascent on a frozen 7B backbone with the number of LM calls held fixed. Fu: six multi-agent configurations vs a single-agent BenchAgent Core, GPT-4.1, three runs of 3,302 instances over ten reasoning, coding and tool-use benchmarks.
- **Headline results:** Dylan: on ALFWorld the full team scored 0.769 vs 0.754 for a single evolved executor (p = 0.80) while using 1.8x the evaluation calls; leave-one-in attributes all realised value to the executor; planner and critic prompts evolved to empty or low-impact text; with 2 to 3x compute the team only matched the single agent; on WebShop the team trended worse. - **Limitations:** Dylan uses a 7B model on non-coding tasks; Fu uses one model (GPT-4.1) and single-turn-ish benchmarks; both preprints.

### 14. Zhou et al. (2026), Agent-as-a-Router, and Paul & Nandy (2026), AgentRouter

## Candidate paradigms

1. **Strong planner, cheap executor (static per-role table).** One model tier per role, planner at or above the worker. Backed by RT-07, RT-08, RT-28, source 23. Best grade: B.
2. **Cheap-first escalation on a verifier signal.** Start every eligible ticket on the cheapest capable model and escalate once on a failed verify, stop or review round. Backed by RT-10, RT-11, RT-25. Best grade: B.
3. **Early-trajectory routing.** Let the cheap worker run K turns and decide from its behaviour (stops, idle, repeated reads) rather than from the ticket text. Backed by RT-11, RT-14, RT-15. Best grade: B.
4. **Scout-then-route (handoff-first).** A cheap read-only scout writes the handoff (touch map, entry points, verify command); the fixer's tier matters less once the handoff exists. Backed by RT-12. Best grade: B.
5. **Per-role effort tiering.** Effort as the main cost lever: medium or low for Opus-class roles, high for Sonnet workers and L reviews, nothing above high. Backed by RT-01, RT-02, RT-03, RT-05, RT-26. Best grade: A.
6. **Repeated cheap attempts with external selection.** Two or three Haiku attempts on S/M tickets, CI and the reviewer pick; never a vote. Backed by RT-13, RT-19, RT-18. Best grade: B.
7. **Cache-first topology.** Stable prefixes per role, no mid-conversation model or effort switch, fresh subagent hand-offs, TTL chosen from the role's wake cadence, Haiku kept under the 100K cliff. Backed by RT-04, RT-23, RT-24, RT-25. Best grade: A (the 61% figure) with C mechanics.
8. **Kind-labelled routing.** The routing rule reads a ticket-kind label as well as size and risk paths. Backed by RT-21, RT-17. Best grade: B.
9. **Transition-gated expensive review.** Deterministic gate first, cheap review per PR, expensive review only at a boundary (risk path, L tier, release). Backed by RT-29 and the existing script gate; cost-controlled by RT-20. Best grade: C.
10. **Minimal-role baseline (the null paradigm).** Orchestrator plus worker plus CI, with every extra role switched on only when the profile shows it earns its cost. Backed by RT-20, RT-30. Best grade: B.
11. **Cost-of-pass as the routing objective.** Route and trial on dollars per merged ticket, with round-1 change rate as the floor, re-evaluated per model generation. Backed by RT-18, RT-22, RT-30. Best grade: B.

## Open questions

- No study measures which model a **reviewer** role needs. Every per-role result is planner/executor; the M-tier-to-Haiku review move in map #513 has no external evidence either way and must rest on the repo's own round-1 change rate.
- The 2026 routing results are preprints with 25 to 100 held-out instances; whether a 53% saving at equal resolve rate survives a random SWE-bench sample, or a private backlog, is unknown.
- Effort curves are per model and recalibrated per generation (Anthropic says so for Sonnet 5.5); no source gives a token multiplier per effort level for the 5.5 generation, so the per-level cost must be measured locally.
- The leaderboards disagree on whether cost includes cache reads and retries; no public number gives cost per resolved issue with caching counted on a current scaffold.
- RT-07 and RT-20 conflict on whether role structure pays; the plausible moderator (heterogeneous models plus a real verifier) is untested as such.
- Whether an early escalation trigger (RT-11) raises cost through false escalations on tickets a Haiku worker would have finished is unmeasured; orchestrate's baseline (19 Haiku tickets, 18 round-1 approvals, 0 escalations) suggests false escalations would dominate at today's S-only rule.
- Repeated cheap attempts (RT-13, RT-19) need a selector; whether CI plus an S-tier reviewer selects as well as the papers' oracles or scored selectors is unknown.
- The Haiku 5.5 100K price cliff interacts with caching in a way no study has priced for an agent whose context grows past the threshold mid-ticket.
